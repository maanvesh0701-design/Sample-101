Under the Hood

What to Expect in the MVP

The Shubhrali MVP is a fork of PocketPal AI — an open-source, MIT-licensed app that runs language models entirely on-device. Shubhrali is that codebase, forked and rebranded, with its own persona built on top of it. It is not a wrapper around someone else's app; it is the app itself, modified and maintained as its own project.

Forked

A fork means taking the actual source code of an existing open-source project and creating an independent copy of it. The code is inspected, modified, rebuilt, and maintained separately going forward — the fork becomes its own project, even though it shares its origins with the original.

Wrapped

A wrapper, by contrast, is a thin layer placed on top of another app or service without touching its underlying code. It typically just reskins the interface or relabels the branding, while the original software keeps running underneath, unmodified — and often still connected to its original servers.

Shubhrali is forked, not wrapped.

Qwen was chosen as the underlying model because it is open source — developed by Alibaba and released publicly under a permissive license that allows modification and commercial use. Its smaller parameter variants are purpose-built to run efficiently on limited hardware, and its training covers strong multilingual ground, Hindi included — which matters directly for an Indian audience.

What comes next builds on this same fork: a Shubhrali-specific fine-tune layered on top of it, followed by the civic utility layer on the roadmap.

Why isn't this a polished app yet?

Because it isn't meant to be, not yet. This is a minimum viable product — its job is to prove the core thesis, not to be a finished consumer app.

The thesis: AI works better with privacy and with real consistency than it does without either. We don't need giant cloud-hosted models for everyday use — and where bigger models genuinely are required, they should come with that same privacy, not the targeted ads and resold data that get sold to us as the only option. The idea that a phone "can't handle this" is mostly hype. Phones are built to handle exactly this.
